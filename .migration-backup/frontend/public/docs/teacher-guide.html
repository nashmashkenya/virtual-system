1. Before you begin

ElimuPawa Classroom is a browser-based virtual classroom. As a teacher, you run sessions from the Teacher meeting room, where you can share camera or screen, manage learners, and use interactive tools (polls, quizzes, chat, Q&A, whiteboard, and more).

What you need

  • A recent version of Chrome, Edge, or Firefox.
  • Stable internet; wired Ethernet is best for long live sessions.
  • Microphone and camera permissions allowed for your browser (and screen-sharing permission when you present slides).
Tip: Open the app using the same URL you give students (for example http://localhost:3000 during local development, or your school’s production URL). That keeps sign-in, cookies, and API calls aligned.

2. Create an account or sign in

  1. From the marketing home page, choose Sign in or Create account, or go directly to /login or /register.
  2. Register with your email and password, or sign in if your administrator already created your account.
  3. After a successful login, teachers are taken to the Teacher meeting room (/teacher).

If you forget your password, use Forgot password on the sign-in page. In local development with debug mode, the app may show a preview reset link so you can complete the flow without email delivery.

4. Create and configure a class session

You need at least one session before you can open the live room. On the teacher dashboard, use Class setup (or equivalent session creation flow) to define:

  • Class name — visible to you and students.
  • Start time — when the lesson is scheduled.
  • Delivery mode:
    • Interactive room — smaller groups, full participation, breakouts, and rich interaction.
    • Broadcast lecture — larger audiences; the interface guides you toward a stable one-to-many flow (for example pairing with an external broadcast such as YouTube Live).
  • Description — optional details shown in the class context.
  • Organization — ties the class to your school or tenant.
  • Program / cohort (optional) — optional grouping for holiday programs or tracks.
  • School roster — manage who belongs to the class; add students from your roster where the product allows.
  • Students & handouts — optional section to attach learners or files for this class.
  • Async materials (links) — links appear on the student dashboard for this class so learners can prepare before or after the live session.

Session rows may show states such as Live now, Upcoming, Paid, or Locked, depending on your configuration and payment rules.

5. Go live: video, screen share, and controls

Select your session, then enter the live teaching experience.

Starting the room

  • If you have no session yet, create one first; the UI prompts you to Create your first live room.
  • Choose Camera mode to teach face-to-face, or Screen share mode to present slides or applications.

Advanced controls (grouped in the UI)

  • Media — mute/unmute, camera on/off, and related AV controls.
  • Stage & share — control what learners see and how content is shared.
  • Recording — start or manage session recording where enabled.
  • Class tools — shortcuts to polls, quizzes, attendance, and other teaching actions.
  • Panels & exit — show or hide side panels and leave the room safely.
Broadcast mode: For very large lectures, follow the on-screen checklist (for example preparing a YouTube live stream in advance and going live several minutes before class).

6. Broadcast lesson checklist (non-technical teachers)

Use this checklist if your class is large and you selected Broadcast lecture. In this mode, students usually watch your video through YouTube Live, while you still manage class tools in ElimuPawa Classroom.

Before class (10 to 20 minutes early)

  1. Open your session on the teacher dashboard and confirm Delivery mode = Broadcast lecture.
  2. In class setup, paste your YouTube live/watch link in Step 2.
  3. If available, use Connect with Google once so the app can show stream status.
  4. Open YouTube Studio and prepare your stream title, thumbnail, and audience settings.
  5. Start your stream in YouTube a few minutes early and check that picture and sound are clear.

At class start

  1. Return to the teacher dashboard and confirm the broadcast readiness checklist is complete.
  2. Click Start class now in ElimuPawa Classroom.
  3. Post one short chat message: "Welcome, please confirm you can hear and see."
  4. Wait for confirmation from several students before beginning the lesson.

During class

  • Teach through your YouTube stream source (camera/slides/encoder) for the main lecture.
  • Use ElimuPawa for engagement: chat, Q&A, polls, quizzes, attendance, and class monitoring.
  • For very large classes, keep it one-to-many; save small-group activity for a separate smaller session.

If something goes wrong

  • No video for students: verify your YouTube link is correct and the stream is actually live.
  • No sound: check your microphone in YouTube/encoder settings, then refresh the teacher page.
  • Students joined late: repost the lesson link and give a quick recap in chat.
Important: In broadcast mode, browser media controls (camera/mic/screen-share) can be limited. This is expected. Your main teaching video comes from YouTube Live.

7. Waiting room, breakouts, and students

Waiting room

When enabled for your session, learners wait in a Waiting room until you approve them. Review the queue and admit people when you are ready so late joiners do not interrupt a sensitive moment.

Breakout rooms

Use Breakout rooms to split the class into smaller groups. You can start a timer, broadcast a short message to all breakouts, and review a summary when groups return to the main room.

Students list

The Students panel lists everyone enrolled in the class. To add or remove learners, use Class setup as directed on screen. Share the room code or join instructions according to your school’s process.

Ops snapshot (where shown)

Some deployments show an Ops snapshot with high-level counters (pending Q&A, active sessions, waiting room, raised hands). Use it as a quick health check during busy periods.

8. Polls, quizzes, Q&A, and raised hands

Live actions

The Live actions area highlights what needs attention during the call (for example marking items as resolved).

Raise hand queue

Students can raise a virtual hand. The Raise hand queue shows who is waiting and may display a Live speaker when someone is given the floor. Clear items as you address them so the queue stays manageable.

Poll control

Create or activate live polls to check understanding or gather opinions. Close or advance polls when you move to the next topic.

Quiz control

Launch short quizzes for comprehension checks. Review outcomes before moving on.

Q&A queue

Text questions may arrive in the Q&A queue. Answer verbally or in chat, then mark items done so duplicates do not pile up. Bulk actions may be available for efficiency.

Chat moderation

Where available, use chat moderation tools to slow down fast-moving chat or remove disruptive messages so the channel stays usable in large classes.

9. Whiteboard and class materials

The collaborative whiteboard lets you sketch and annotate during class. Changes typically save to the server; watch for save status indicators if your network is slow. You may have options such as locking the board or present mode depending on your deployment.

Resources and async materials you attach in class setup help students find PDFs, links, and reference content from their own dashboard.

10. Payments and settings

Payments

Visit Payments (/payments) for fee-related screens. Some sessions may show as Paid or Locked until payment rules are satisfied—follow your school’s policy and the on-screen messaging.

Settings

Under Settings (/settings), adjust dark mode, notification preferences, mobile-oriented options, and security-related messaging shown in the product. Changes apply across your classroom experience for a consistent look and feel.

11. Tips for a smooth lesson

  • Join five to ten minutes early to test camera, mic, and screen share.
  • Keep the raise-hand and Q&A queues short by addressing items in batches.
  • For broadcast mode, rehearse the external stream (for example YouTube) before the first real audience.
  • Use breakout rooms for peer practice; set a visible timer so groups know when to return.
  • End the session from the room controls so students see a clear conclusion.
